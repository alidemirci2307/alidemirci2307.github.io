Dopamine detox: a practical guide to getting your attention back

Endless feeds and short videos are built to keep you scrolling. A dopamine detox is a simple habit reset: you cut back on quick, effortless rewards so that reading, training, studying and real conversations feel rewarding again.

How to do a dopamine detox in 7 days

  1. Find your triggers. Check your screen time and write down the two or three apps you open without thinking.
  2. Put a pause in front of them. A few seconds of friction before an app opens is often enough to break the reflex.
  3. Set daily limits. Give each trigger app a small daily budget instead of trying to quit it overnight.
  4. Protect fixed hours. Block feeds during work or study blocks and in the hour before sleep.
  5. Replace, don't just remove. Plan a short walk, a page of a book or a glass of water for the moments you would normally scroll.
  6. Track your streak. Seeing progress day by day keeps motivation up after the first enthusiasm fades.
  7. Review after a week. Keep the limits that worked, loosen the ones that were unrealistic.

Frequently asked questions

Is a dopamine detox scientifically proven?

"Dopamine detox" is a popular name for reducing high-stimulation habits, not a medical treatment. The useful part is behavioural: less compulsive checking and more time for activities you value. If you are struggling with anxiety, sleep or addiction, talk to a professional.

How long should it last?

Start small: one phone-free hour a day or one low-screen evening a week, then try a 7-day challenge with daily limits on your most distracting apps.

Do I have to delete social media?

No. Most people do better with limits and pauses than with an all-or-nothing ban that is hard to keep.
